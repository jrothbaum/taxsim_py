    Comparison with TAXSIM and PolicyEngine-US

    This page is generated by scripts/build_comparison_report.py. It compares taxsim-py with the TAXSIM executable and with PolicyEngine-US on real households, for every state and year.

    Households

    The households are the 200 randomly drawn CPS ASEC tax units per state from the 2011 public-use file (income year 2010), 10,200 units in all, built with taxsim_py.prep.cps_asec_tax_units. For each law year from 1960 to 2026 the same units are run with every dollar input multiplied by the ratio of that year's consumer price index to 2010's (2025 and 2026 use assumed 2.7% and 2.5% inflation), so each year sees a realistic mix at that year's prices. Before 1977 only the federal return is calculated. TAXSIM itself is run one state and one year at a time, because its results for a record depend on the records that ran before it (variables left over from another state's routine; Maryland 1977-1984 differs by about $90 per household when mixed with other states). A household "agrees" when the tax differs by $1 or less. Federal rows count all units; state rows count the states with a wage income tax.

    Against TAXSIM

    In short: in TAXSIM mode, every CPS household of every state for every year 1977-2026 (4,206,050 state-year calculations) was checked against TAXSIM, and 2,181 differ in state tax by more than $1 (99.95% agree). Federal tax differs by more than $1 for 3,154 households, 1,079 of them the 2008 recovery rebate (an open TAXSIM issue). The state-by-state counts are in docs/data/cps_full_state_check/summary.csv.

    TAXSIM mode reproduces the executable, bugs included. Statutory mode follows the law, so it is expected to differ where TAXSIM is wrong or out of date; each difference is listed in TAXSIM errors and corrections.

    Over all states and years since 1977, 99.9% of households agree with TAXSIM's state tax in TAXSIM mode (99.8% within 2 cents), and 75.2% in statutory mode.

    Against PolicyEngine-US

    Statutory mode against PolicyEngine-US's TAXSIM interface for 2022-2026, on 40 units per state (PolicyEngine is much slower). Only households whose federal tax agrees within $1 count, so the comparison is of state logic. PolicyEngine's adapter ignores dep6 and turns the dependent counts into children of default ages, so credits that depend on a child's age differ in ways that are not errors in either model. Where the two disagree, the form or statute decides; the known cases are in Comparison with PolicyEngine-US.
