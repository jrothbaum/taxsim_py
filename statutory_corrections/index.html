    TAXSIM errors and corrections

    This page lists the apparent errors in NBER TAXSIM that turned up while porting it, what taxsim-py does about each one, and the other deliberate differences from the compiled program. Each error was judged from the Fortran source and from tests against the compiled program. None has been confirmed with TAXSIM's authors.

    Line numbers refer to taxsim_2024_09_21.f, and "the executable" means taxsim2024.exe.

    The taxsim-py column says how each error is handled:

    • Statutory: calculation_mode="statutory", the default, follows the law, and calculation_mode="taxsim" reproduces TAXSIM for replication and oracle tests. Each correction has an ID and a switch, named in the row, in src/taxsim_py/behavior.py; formula code reads that profile, never the mode string. Tests are rows of tests/statutory_correction_table.py, run by tests/test_statutory_corrections.py (federal payroll tax: tests/test_calculation_modes.py). Years after 2021 run only in statutory mode for states, so a fix confined to those years needs no switch.
    • Both modes: TAXSIM's number is wrong or out of date for its year, so the published value is used in both modes, as for parameter updates (new years, dated tables). scripts/validate_states.py counts these state years as expected differences (KNOWN_ORACLE_DIVERGENT_STATE_YEARS); scripts/validate_federal.py marks the federal cases oracle_divergent.
    • Projection: the error is in TAXSIM's projection of 2021 law to later years. Taxsim mode keeps it; statutory mode uses actual law for those years.
    • No effect and Not changed: the error cannot change a result, or the right rule needs information TAXSIM has no input for.

    Every entry needs a reason, an implementation reference, and tests for both behaviors. Differences that are not TAXSIM errors (extensions and actual law for 2022 on), how the executable processes a file, and entries found not to be errors are listed at the end.

    Federal

    Payroll tax

    The first four came from a 10,000-unit 2021 CPS sample, where together they explain all 145 payroll differences from Tax-Calculator. Statutory mode matches Tax-Calculator to the cent on the focused records and leaves 3 residuals (Tax-Calculator applies the $400 threshold to combined spousal income; the port applies it per spouse, as the Schedule SE instructions require). Code: engine.payroll_tax.taxsim_payroll; tests: the single_high_se, wage_se_cap, below_minimum and losses records.

    All Self-employment tax is charged from the first dollar Statutory, FED-PAYROLL-003: none when 92.35% of net business income is under $400, per spouse (Schedule SE instructions). enforce_schedule_se_minimum
    All 22300-22330 A business loss gives negative self-employment tax, below wage FICA or below zero: $50,000 of wages and a $10,000 loss gives $6,237 instead of $7,650 Statutory, FED-PAYROLL-004: business results netted per taxpayer, floored at zero, allocated over positive items; a loss does not reduce the Additional Medicare base (Schedule SE, Form 8959). prevent_negative_self_employment_tax

    Federal marginal rate that states read

    Some states before 2001 (Nebraska, North Dakota, Rhode Island and Vermont report it as their bracket rate) use TAXSIM's analytic federal marginal rate (comnew(72)). These errors affect only that rate, not federal tax. Statutory mode corrects each, FED-RATE-001 (correct_analytic_federal_rate), except the Social Security term: the branch TAXSIM always takes there is the correct formula, and the alternative it skips would count the EITC slope twice.

    Years Line What TAXSIM does
    1977-1978 23705-23724, 23874 The alternative capital gains rate is 0 for moderate gains, so Nebraska 1977 reports 0 for such couples
    1979-1982 24100-24135 The minimum-tax rate is used only if the zero-bracket amount is 0, which never happens; 1982 nets to minus 60% of the minimum-tax rate for some itemizers
    1981 23628-23632 The 1.25% rate-reduction credit is overwritten on the next line, so it reaches the tax but not the rate
    1993-1996 tax93, tax94 The rate ignores the 28% cap on gains (1993) or uses income less gains even when that is not the cheaper method (1994-1996)
    1994 on 25925-25937 The Social Security term compares a percent with a fraction, so the alternative formula never runs (not changed, see above)
    1998 on 25733, 25758, 25972 The child tax credit adds .05, a fraction, to a rate in percent: +0.05 points
    All 25946 The elderly-credit term subtracts a percent from 50 inside a fraction
    All 27860-27880 The EITC slope is set before the disqualification tests, so it stays in the rate when no credit is paid

    Other differences from TAXSIM

    These are statutory-mode differences that do not fix a TAXSIM error.

    • WA-001 (extension). TAXSIM calls no Washington tax routine, so state tax is 0. Statutory mode adds the Working Families Tax Credit (RCW 82.08.0206), a refundable $300-$1,290 by number of children, phased out below the EITC ceiling with a $50 floor, as negative state tax, through 2025. The 7% capital gains excise tax over $250,000 is not modelled: TAXSIM's ltcg cannot separate the exempt real estate and retirement gains. PolicyEngine taxes all ltcg, so large-gain households differ by about 7% of the gain above the threshold. Switch: add_washington_working_families_credit.
    • CT-001 (2022+). The personal-credit worksheet is read as discrete AGI bands (the first threshold reached), not TAXSIM's linear interpolation between rows (2022 CT-1040 instructions).
    • OR-002. The 12% under-three EITC match uses children_under_3, and the care credit cap uses dep13.

    Age-65+ rules in actual-law years (STATE-2022-AGED)

    Statutory mode only, 2022 onward; each follows the state's form or statute. AZ senior property-tax credit limited to taxes paid (all statutory years, AZ-001); IN elderly credit as a step schedule; NM low-income rebate tables; KY pension cap per spouse (all statutory years, KY-001), combined filing, and poverty-guideline family-size credit; OH joint-filing credit for pension income; NJ pension exclusion phase-down; CA credit for age 65+; MN subtraction for the elderly or disabled; ND marriage credit with shared pensions; MI tier-three standard deduction and phased-in retirement subtraction; CO high-income add-backs; RI $20,000 retirement exclusion from 2023 with indexed limits; WV family credit tested on federal AGI; and smaller items in IA, GA, MT, LA, AR, IL, AL and UT.     2025 (2025-LAW)

    Federal: child credit $2,200, the senior deduction, and the SALT cap phase-down (src/taxsim_py/parameters/national/*). States: see the PolicyEngine comparison.
