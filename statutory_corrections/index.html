    Statutory corrections

    This ledger records the deliberate differences from the compiled NBER TAXSIM. calculation_mode="statutory" (the default) applies them and uses the canonical parameter tables; calculation_mode="taxsim" keeps the compiled model's behavior, including its known bugs, for replication and oracle tests. The switches live in src/taxsim_py/behavior.py; formula code reads that profile, never the mode string. Every correction needs a reason, an implementation reference, and tests for both behaviors. Parameter-only updates (new years, dated tables) apply in every mode. The full list of apparent TAXSIM errors, including those reproduced in both modes, is in Known TAXSIM errors.

    Federal payroll tax

    These four came from a 10,000-unit 2021 CPS sample, where together they explain all 145 payroll differences from Tax-Calculator. Statutory mode matches Tax-Calculator to the cent on the focused records and leaves 3 residuals (Tax-Calculator applies the $400 threshold to combined spousal income; the port applies it per spouse, as the Schedule SE instructions require). Code: engine.payroll_tax.taxsim_payroll; tests: tests/test_calculation_modes.py (the single_high_se, wage_se_cap, below_minimum and losses records).

    Federal income tax

    Tests for these and the state corrections below are rows of tests/statutory_correction_table.py, run by tests/test_statutory_corrections.py.

    • FED-INCOME-001 (1987-1997). TAXSIM applies no nonrefundable credits before 1998 but reports a credit total (comnew(58)) that is tax before credits when the elderly credit exceeds it and 0 otherwise (a 68-year-old with $8,000 of wages in 1995 has a $712.50 credit and is reported $97.50). States that read it (LA, UT, OR, ND, AL, AZ) get the wrong federal base. Statutory mode reports the real federal_elder. Found by probing the executable. Switch: count_pre_1998_nonrefundable_credits_correctly; code: federal._net_tax.
    • FED-INCOME-002 (2022+). The Credit for Other Dependents is computed (500*max(0, data(8)-ideps)) and then never added to the credit total after 2020, so adult and age-17 dependents earn nothing. Statutory mode gives $500 per dependent who is not a CTC-qualifying child, with the CTC phaseout (IRC 24(h)(4)-(5)); about 100 CPS units per year. Switch: allow_other_dependent_credit_after_2021; code: federal._credits.
    • FED-INCOME-003. Social Security provisional income adds transfers, which the input reader stores where tax-exempt interest used to be (data(41)). The law adds tax-exempt interest, which TAXSIM has no input for. Statutory mode adds nothing; the same applies to the Iowa (2014 on) and Montana worksheets. Switch: exclude_transfers_from_provisional_income.
    • FED-INCOME-004 (1987-1999). The AMT is computed but added to tax only from
    • Switch: add_amt_before_2000.
    • FED-INCOME-005 (1987-1997). The child and dependent care credit and the credit for the elderly are computed but never subtracted (the code sits inside the 1998 child tax credit block). Statutory mode applies both and reports them as the nonrefundable credit total, which supersedes FED-INCOME-001. Switch: apply_nonrefundable_credits_before_1998.
    • FED-INCOME-006. The elderly credit's AGI threshold is $7,500 for a joint return with one spouse 65 or older; IRC 22(d) sets $10,000 for any joint return. Switch: use_joint_elderly_credit_threshold.
    • FED-INCOME-007 (1991-1992). AMT income adds back the itemized-deduction limitation, from which the AMT was exempt from 1991. Switch: exempt_amt_from_itemized_limit_1991_1992.
    • FED-INCOME-008 (1990 on). The young-filer AMT exemption cap applies to anyone under the age limit; IRC 59(j) limits it to children subject to the kiddie tax. Statutory mode applies it to dependent filers only. Switch: cap_amt_exemption_for_dependents_only.
    • FED-INCOME-009 (1998-2000). The additional child credit for three or more children reads a payroll figure that is never set, so it is always $0. Statutory mode pays the unused credit up to the employee share of payroll tax (and half of self-employment tax) less the EITC (IRC 24(d) as enacted by P.L. 105-34). Switch: allow_additional_child_credit_before_2001.
    • FED-INCOME-010 (before 1987). Business, professional and S corporation income are left out of income (in 1960-1986 law). Switch: include_business_income_before_1987.
    • FED-INCOME-011 (1979). The dividend exclusion is read from an input that is never set, so it is $0; the law kept $100 ($200 joint). The states that add the exclusion back read the same rule (engine.state.federal_dividend_exclusion_limit). Switch: apply_1979_dividend_exclusion.
    • FED-INCOME-012 (1977-1986). The credit for the elderly is capped at an input that is never set, so it is $0. Statutory mode pays 15% of $2,500 ($3,750 for a couple both 65 or older; from 1984 $5,000 and $7,500) less nontaxable Social Security and half of AGI over $7,500 ($10,000 joint) (IRC 37 as amended in 1976; IRC 22 from 1984). Switch: apply_elderly_credit_before_1987.
    • FED-INCOME-013 (1983-1986). The minimum-tax base leaves out qualified housing interest, which the law allowed. Switch: deduct_mortgage_interest_from_1983_minimum_tax.
    • FED-INCOME-014 (1977-1978). Itemizers' preference income for the maximum tax on earned income includes wages and unemployment compensation. Switch: exclude_wages_from_1977_preference_income.
    • FED-RATE-001 (1977-2000). TAXSIM's analytic federal marginal rate (comnew(72)), which Nebraska, North Dakota, Rhode Island and Vermont report as their bracket rate, has several slips: no rate for moderate 1977-1978 gains, no 1979-1982 minimum-tax rate, the 1981 rate reduction overwritten, the 1993-1996 gains cap ignored or misapplied, the child tax credit's 5 points added as 0.05, the elderly credit's slope mixing a percent into a fraction, and the EITC slope kept when the credit is disqualified. Statutory mode corrects each; tax is unaffected. Switch: correct_analytic_federal_rate.

    Not changed: the 2021 married-separate EITC (the law requires living apart for six months, which TAXSIM has no input for), and the analytic rate's Social Security term (the branch TAXSIM always takes is the correct formula).

    Rules shared by several states

    • STATE-CCC-001 (before 1987). District of Columbia (1982-1986), Iowa (1977-1981, 1986), Minnesota and New York credits are shares of the federal child care credit, which TAXSIM's early federal routine never fills in. Statutory mode uses the 1977-1986 federal credit. Switch: read_federal_child_care_credit_before_1987.
    • STATE-HHY-001. TAXSIM's household income (data(159)) leaves out self-employment income. Switch: include_self_employment_in_household_income.
    • STATE-HHY-002. Household income includes a leftover $1 from the input reader, and the state worksheet (idtl=2) is printed from the one-cent marginal-rate run. Statutory mode drops the $1 and reports the base calculation. Switches: drop_household_income_record_adjustment, report_base_state_worksheet.
    • STATE-SOCSEC-001. The payroll tax states deduct (socsec; Kansas, Missouri, Vermont) charges 1.45% on wages above the Social Security base in every year. Statutory mode stops at the Medicare base (the same base before 1991). Switch: cap_medicare_in_state_payroll_deduction.

    California

    • CA-001 (1979-1986). Taxable unemployment compensation is never in total income but is subtracted again in the AGI adjustment ($30,000 of wages and $4,000 of unemployment gives AGI $26,000). Statutory mode stops subtracting it. Switch: include_unemployment_in_california_total_income.
    • CA-002 (1987+). The minimum-tax base (alminy) subtracts positive self-employment and Schedule E income; Schedule P keeps them (a 2004 couple's rent lowers their tax by 7 cents a dollar). Statutory mode keeps them. Switch: include_business_and_rental_income_in_california_minimum_tax.
    • CA-004 (2019+). The Young Child Tax Credit is computed from earnings without testing for a child under six (catax lines 2911-2919). Statutory mode requires one (R&TC 17052.1) and pays one credit per return, where TAXSIM multiplies by the number of young children. Switches: require_young_child_for_california_yctc, pay_california_yctc_per_young_child.
    • CA-005 / NJ-001 (CA 2018+, NJ 2020+). State childless credits start at age 18, not the federal 25. TAXSIM pays California at any age (a 15-year-old with $5,000 of wages gets $208.66) and gives New Jersey only the federal age test. California has no maximum age (R&TC 17052). New Jersey requires 21 and under 65 for 2020 and 18 with no maximum from 2021, and pays a childless filer who meets every federal rule except age a flat amount, 40% of the federal childless maximum rounded ($224 for 2022, $601 for 2021; NJ-1040 line 58); others get 40% of their federal credit. An unreported age passes. Switch: apply_state_childless_eitc_minimum_age; code: states.ca, states.nj.
    • CA-006 (2018+). TAXSIM starts California itemized deductions from the federal SALT-capped total. California never adopted the cap (Schedule CA (540)): real estate and other taxes are deductible in full, its own income tax not at all. Switch: ignore_federal_salt_cap_in_california.

    Other states

    • AL-001 (2013+). The itemized deduction for federal payroll tax takes half of FICA and then adds the employee-only Additional Medicare tax, which TAXSIM's FICA total already includes, so it is counted one and a half times. Statutory mode counts it once. Switch: count_additional_medicare_once_in_alabama_fica_deduction.
    • AZ-001. The senior property tax credit: the income test excludes Social Security (which TAXSIM's household income includes) and the credit is limited to property tax paid (A.R.S. 43-1072). Applied in every statutory year. Switch: limit_arizona_property_credit_per_statute.
    • IL-001 (2017+). No exemption allowance when income exceeds $250,000 ($500,000 joint) (35 ILCS 5/204(g)); TAXSIM allows it at any income. Switch: apply_illinois_exemption_income_limit.
    • KS-001 (2021+). TAXSIM stops letting Kansas filers itemize after 2020. Kansas itemizing follows the federal choice in every year (K-40 Schedule A). Switch: allow_kansas_itemizing_after_2020.
    • KY-001 (1995+). The pension exclusion cap is per taxpayer (KRS 141.010(10)), with a joint return's pensions split evenly; TAXSIM applies one cap per return. Applied in every statutory year. Switch: apply_kentucky_pension_cap_per_taxpayer.
    • MA-001. TAXSIM deducts only the primary earner's payroll tax, capped at $2,000 for the return. Each spouse deducts their own employee payroll tax up to $2,000 (Form 1 lines 11a/11b), so a couple earning $50,000 and $75,000 gets the second $2,000 ($100 of tax at 5%). Switch: deduct_massachusetts_employee_payroll_tax_per_spouse.
    • CO-001 (2022+). TAXSIM subtracts pensions plus Social Security only when someone is 65 or older, under one combined cap. Each taxpayer subtracts pension income up to $20,000 (55-64) or $24,000 (65+), less their Social Security subtraction; those 65+ subtract all taxable Social Security, those 55-64 within the cap (all of it from 2025 when federal AGI is at most $75,000, $95,000 joint; HB24-1142). Pension and Social Security inputs are split equally between spouses. Sources: C.R.S. 39-22-104(4)(f)-(g), DR 0104AD.
    • HI-003. TAXSIM's pension subtraction reads data(72), which the input reader never sets (pensions are data(20)), so Hawaii never excludes a pension. Statutory mode excludes pensions. Switch: exclude_hawaii_pensions.
    • WA-001 (extension, not a correction). TAXSIM calls no Washington tax routine, so state tax is 0. Statutory mode adds the Working Families Tax Credit (RCW 82.08.0206), a refundable $300-$1,290 by number of children, phased out below the EITC ceiling with a $50 floor, as negative state tax. The 7% capital gains excise tax over $250,000 is not modelled: TAXSIM's ltcg cannot separate the exempt real estate and retirement gains. PolicyEngine taxes all ltcg, so large-gain households differ by about 7% of the gain above the threshold. Switch: add_washington_working_families_credit.
    • CT-001 (2022+). The personal-credit worksheet is read as discrete AGI bands (the first threshold reached), not TAXSIM's linear interpolation between rows (2022 CT-1040 instructions).
    • DC-002 (2015+). The EITC with-child track uses the age-qualified child count dep18, not depx > 0. Switch: use_age_qualified_children_for_dc_eitc.
    • DC-003 (2015+). The childless credit's investment income counts positive capital gains as well as interest and dividends (D-40 EITC checklist); TAXSIM counts interest and dividends only. Switch: count_capital_gains_in_dc_childless_eitc_investment_income.
    • NY-001 (2006-2022). Children under 4 do not qualify for the Empire State child credit before 2023 (Tax Law 606(c-1)); TAXSIM has no ages. Statutory mode removes children_under_4 from the 2018-2022 rebuilt credit (the 2006-2017 credit still counts them). Switch: exclude_children_under_4_from_ny_empire_credit.
    • OR-003 (2016+). The working family household and dependent care credit counts only children under 13 (ORS 315.264, IRC 21(b)); TAXSIM counts every dependent. Switch: count_only_children_under_13_for_oregon_dependent_care.
    • OR-002. The 12% under-three EITC match uses children_under_3, and the care credit cap uses dep13.

    Published values that replace TAXSIM's (both modes)

    Where TAXSIM's own table is wrong for its year, the published value is used in both modes, as for parameter updates.

    • AR (2004). Rate thresholds from the DFA 2004 Indexed Tax Brackets ($3,400, $6,800, $10,300, $17,100, $28,500); TAXSIM reuses the 1977 thresholds.
    • CA (2001, 2018). Schedule P (540) line 22 exemptions: 2001 head of household $48,114 and married separate $32,075 (TAXSIM repeats 2000's), 2018 married separate $47,685 (TAXSIM $44,685).
    • Federal (2022). The single 20% capital-gains threshold is $459,750 (Rev. Proc. 2021-45); TAXSIM's regular-tax copy has $459,650.
    • MD (2019-2021). Standard deduction limits from the Resident Booklet, Instruction 16: single $1,500-$2,250, $1,550-$2,300, $1,550-$2,350; joint $3,050-$4,550, $3,100-$4,650, $3,100-$4,700 (TAXSIM fills its arrays out of order).
    • MN (2009). The head-of-household AMT exclusion equals single, $49,860 (TAXSIM has 2010's $49,960).
    • NE (1996, 1997, 2017). Additional-tax bands $287,950 (1996 single) and $588,700 (1997 joint), which step by fixed amounts every year (TAXSIM $297,950 and $575,950); the 2017 inflation factor 1.046 used by every other state (TAXSIM 1.08).
    • WV (2011). The family credit limit for five exemptions is $26,170; the limits step by $3,820 (TAXSIM $28,170).

    scripts/validate_states.py counts these state years as expected differences (KNOWN_ORACLE_DIVERGENT_STATE_YEARS), as scripts/validate_federal.py does for 2023.

    Dated 2022+ state tables (STATE-DATED)

    TAXSIM's tables stop at 2021 and its later years scale 2021 law. Statutory mode runs actual law for 2022-2025 from PolicyEngine-US parameters checked against state forms; taxsim mode keeps the projection. Boundaries are the provisions TAXSIM has no input for (see the PolicyEngine comparison).

    State (IDs) Dated values
    AR (AR-001) Standard deduction, rate and subtraction schedules, low-income tax tables, 2022-2023 inflationary-relief credit
    CA (CA-003) Standard deductions, rate thresholds, exemption credits, CalEITC maximums, Young Child Tax Credit amounts, AMT exemptions and phaseout starts (Schedule P (540))
    DC (DC-001) Schedule H maximum, AGI tiers, elderly threshold; rent counts at 20% added to property tax
    HI (HI-002) 2024 doubled standard deductions, 2023-2024 Food/Excise credit tables, refundable 40% EITC from 2023 (Acts 46, 114, 163)
    NH (NH-001) Interest-and-dividends rate 4% (2023), 3% (2024), 0 (2025); exemptions unchanged
    OH (OH-001) Brackets, zero-bracket thresholds, exemptions, 30% EITC match (2024 IT-1040)
    OK (OK-001) Single/separate and joint/head-of-household rate tables (0.25%-4.75%)
    OR (OR-001) Brackets, standard deductions, federal-tax-subtraction limits, exemption credits
    Others CO, CT, GA, ID, IL, IN, KY, MI, NC, PA, UT and the remaining income-tax states: see the PolicyEngine comparison

    Age-65+ rules in actual-law years (STATE-2022-AGED)

    Statutory mode only, 2022 onward; each follows the state's form or statute. AZ senior property-tax credit limited to taxes paid (all statutory years, AZ-001); IN elderly credit as a step schedule; NM low-income rebate tables; KY pension cap per spouse (all statutory years, KY-001), combined filing, and poverty-guideline family-size credit; OH joint-filing credit for pension income; NJ pension exclusion phase-down; CA credit for age 65+; MN subtraction for the elderly or disabled; ND marriage credit with shared pensions; MI tier-three standard deduction and phased-in retirement subtraction; CO high-income add-backs; RI $20,000 retirement exclusion from 2023 with indexed limits; WV family credit tested on federal AGI; and smaller items in IA, GA, MT, LA, AR, IL, AL and UT. TAXSIM has one household pensions total, so per-spouse caps and spouse-income tests treat it as split evenly.

    2025 (2025-LAW)

    Federal: child credit $2,200, the senior deduction, and the SALT cap phase-down (src/taxsim_py/parameters/national/*). States: see the PolicyEngine comparison. Provisions that depart from TAXSIM's conventions (Maine's and DC's refusal of the larger federal standard deduction, South Carolina's addbacks) are statutory mode only; parameter updates apply in every mode. Washington's credit extends to 2025.
